The dryer stopped producing heat. This was a later, separate problem from the belt-related no-spin issue I wrote about in [The Dryer Stopped Spinning]({{ site.baseurl }}/fixing-dryer-not-spinning/). This time, the heating element had broken.

## Diagnosis

I used a multimeter to check continuity across the heating element, thermostat, and fuse. The open circuit was at the heater. After removing it, I could also see that the heating coil was broken.

## Getting to the Heating Element

To access the heater, I removed the top panel using the two screws at the back. That exposed the screws for the front panel, which I then removed. I then detached the belt and removed the drum.

With the drum out, I could reach the heating element, thermostat, and fuse. A cover around the heater assembly, part of the dryer’s airflow path, also had to come off to access the coil.

The rectangular duct on the bottom right of the image houses the heater assembly.

![The opened dryer interior showing the heater assembly and airflow components]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/dryer-heater-assembly-interior.jpg)

The dryer was very linty inside, so having it open was a good opportunity to vacuum out the lint. I’m describing the sequence I followed on this dryer; access and construction can differ between models.

## Replacing the Element

I looked up a replacement part based on the dryer model and replaced the broken element.

## Test and Outcome

After reassembly, I ran a test cycle and confirmed that the dryer was producing heat again.

This repair was a later no-heat failure, separate from the earlier belt-related no-spin repair. In the first repair, the heating circuit tested fine and the belt had come off; this time, the broken heating coil was the fault.
